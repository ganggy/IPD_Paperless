- 1802 William Heberden, first description of ARF in termed Ischuria Renalis
- 1909 William Osler, named Acute Bright’s disease, as a consequence of toxic agents, pregnancy,
burns, trauma or operations on the kidneys
- 1918 William MacNider used the term Acute Kidney Injury in a situation of acute mercury poisoning
- WW-I, the syndrome was named War nephritis
- WW-II Bywaters and Beall published in named Crush syndrome
- Acute Tubular Necrosis, histological evidence for patchy necrosis renal tubule at autopsy, were used
interchangeably with ARF
- 1951 Hormer Smith is credited for the introduction of the term Acute Renal Failure
- 2002 survey revealed at least 35 definitions in the scientific literature
- 2004 ARF redefined and RIFLE used the preferred term of Acute Kidney Injury
